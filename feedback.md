## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document records the efforts made to collect feedback on PivotOS, a browser-based operations management application designed to help users respond to unexpected project disruptions, understand their impact, and recover their work through dependency-aware planning.

The goal is to evaluate whether PivotOS solves a meaningful problem, whether its purpose is easy to understand, whether its interface is intuitive, and which improvements would make it more useful to real users.

This document distinguishes between feedback requests sent, responses actually received, and improvements identified for future development.

## 2. Project Overview

PivotOS helps users manage tasks, resources, dependencies, schedules, and operational disruptions in one interactive workspace.

Its core workflow allows users to model a project, simulate a disruption, identify affected tasks, calculate a revised plan, compare the original and proposed schedules, and review the reasoning behind the changes.

### Problem Being Solved

Traditional project plans can become unreliable when deadlines change, team members become unavailable, resources run short, or urgent work appears. Users need a way to understand the consequences of these disruptions and determine how to recover without losing visibility into their work.

### Proposed Solution

PivotOS combines task and resource management, disruption simulation, dependency-aware scheduling, impact analysis, recovery planning, plan comparison, decision history, and offline recovery simulation in a single application.

### What Makes PivotOS Different

Rather than merely displaying project status or disruption alerts, PivotOS focuses on what happens next: identifying downstream effects, evaluating recovery options, comparing plans, and helping users understand the impact of operational changes.

## 5. Feedback Questions for Testers

People testing PivotOS can provide feedback on the following areas.

### A. First Impressions

- What do you think PivotOS does after opening it?
- Is the main purpose clear without reading the full documentation?
- Which feature stands out first?
- Is anything confusing on the initial screen?

### B. Usability and Navigation

- Can you find the task management and resource management features?
- Can you understand how to simulate a disruption?
- Is it clear which tasks are affected by a disruption?
- Is comparing the original plan and recovery plan straightforward?
- Are the labels, buttons, and status indicators understandable?

### C. Functionality

- Can you create and manage tasks?
- Can you configure dependencies and priorities?
- Does disruption simulation produce understandable impact information?
- Are recovery options and schedule changes clearly presented?
- Does the decision history help explain previous actions?
- Does the offline recovery simulator behave as expected?

### D. Practical Value

- Would PivotOS help you manage a real project?
- Which type of user would benefit most from it?
- Which feature would you use most frequently?
- What important capability is missing?
- Would you use it again after the first trial?

### E. Bugs and Improvements

- Did any action fail or produce an unexpected result?
- Did you encounter layout issues or confusing messages?
- Was any information difficult to interpret?
- What is the single most important improvement you would recommend?

## 6. How Feedback Will Be Evaluated

Feedback will be reviewed according to the following priorities:

1. **Critical issues:** Broken workflows, data loss, incorrect calculations, or features that prevent normal use.
2. **Usability issues:** Confusing navigation, unclear instructions, or difficulty understanding results.
3. **Functional gaps:** Missing capabilities that prevent users from completing the intended workflow.
4. **Value improvements:** Suggestions that make PivotOS more useful in realistic operational situations.
5. **Visual refinements:** Improvements to readability, layout, accessibility, and interaction feedback.

Repeated observations from different testers will be considered more significant than isolated preferences, while serious functional issues will be investigated even if reported by only one person.
